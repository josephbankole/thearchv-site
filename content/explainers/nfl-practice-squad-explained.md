An NFL club dresses 53 players and needs about 70 to get through a season. The practice squad is the gap, and it is where a large part of the league actually lives.

## The size

A practice squad holds 16 players. A club in the International Player Pathway programme may carry a 17th, with the restriction that the pathway player cannot be signed to the regular-season roster, though since 2024 he can be elevated for up to three games.

The composition is capped by experience. A squad may carry up to 10 rookies or players with limited accrued service, and up to 6 unrestricted veterans. That veteran allowance is the modern part of the rule. The practice squad used to be a development shelf for young players; it now doubles as a holding pattern for experienced ones a club wants available.

## Elevations

An elevation moves a practice squad player onto the gameday roster for a single match without signing him to the 53.

Each player may be elevated three times a season. A fourth appearance requires an actual signing to the active roster. A club may elevate a maximum of two practice squad players for any given regular-season or postseason game.

That three-strike limit is the whole design. It gives a club cover for injuries in the days before a game without letting it run a permanent 55-man roster.

## The money

In 2026 a practice squad player with two or fewer accrued seasons earns $13,750 a week, per Pro Football Network and Sports Illustrated. The veteran range runs from roughly $18,350 to $22,850 a week.

Elevation pays differently. A player elevated for a regular-season game is paid one seventeenth of the active-roster minimum for a player with his credited seasons, for that week.

These structures are fixed through 2030, when the current collective bargaining agreement expires.

## The part that makes it precarious

A practice squad player is not your player in the way a rostered player is.

Any other club may sign him to its active 53-man roster at any time, and he is free to go. The original club cannot block it and receives nothing. It can only offer a roster spot of its own, which means giving up a place on the 53.

That is why a club with a promising practice squad quarterback spends the season quietly worrying, and why "signed off another team's practice squad" appears in the transactions column every week of the year.

## Why it exists at all

American football damages the people who play it, at a rate no other major sport approaches. A club that cannot replace a guard on Thursday cannot field a team on Sunday.

The practice squad exists so that replacement is already in the building, already knows the playbook, and costs about a thousandth of a starter. It is the sport's answer to a problem the sport itself creates.
